# Commercial validation and lead research

The product is **not validated**. This document is a sales-learning operating guide, not permission to run mass unsolicited messages automatically.

## Research pool and qualified set

**Research target:** up to 500 real companies. **First outreach experiment:** 200 qualified companies over 10 business days (20/day) **if** prospect/contact verification, platform rules and deliverability permit. Do not treat 500 as an email quota.

Prioritize the US, followed by UK where lawful B2B outreach is appropriate. Evaluate other jurisdictions individually: UK GDPR/PECR, US CAN-SPAM, Canadian CASL, South African POPIA and EU local marketing rules are not interchangeable. Seek advice for jurisdictions with more restrictive unsolicited email rules.

## Qualification requirements (minimum)

- Independent **third-party distributor** of electrical, automation or electronic parts.
- Ideally 5–50 employees; up to 100 with evidence of relevant quoting team.
- Verifiably active official website and non-duplicate domain.
- **Direct first-party evidence** of RFQ/BOM/part list/manual quotation intake; record URL and exact relevant wording.
- Named relevant owner, president, operations or inside-sales leader; **actual LinkedIn URL** if verified.
- Publicly listed corporate business contact email if available; do not construct addresses from naming conventions.
- Exclude big corporations, manufacturers without multi-brand distribution, 100% instant-checkout retailers, irrelevant businesses and unverified profiles.

Store prospect contact information privately in a compliant CRM, **not** in this public repository. Label any unverified fields blank.

## Suggested score (0–100)

- 30 — independent distributor in relevant component vertical.
- 25 — direct website RFQ/BOM/manual quote evidence.
- 15 — clear small/medium company size with attributable source.
- 15 — named relevant decision-maker with verified professional profile.
- 10 — multi-line/spec-heavy sales context (not only one-click e-commerce).
- 5 — hiring, quote turnaround promise or other genuine trigger.

70+ means **research-qualified**, not "has pain" and not "ready to buy".

## What we need to learn from five discovery calls

1. How many RFQs arrive weekly? Which percentage are multi-line?
2. Do they come as structured MPN lists, PDFs, Excel, photos or prose?
3. Describe the path from request to approved quotation, including ERP.
4. Which exact step takes longest: extraction, lookup, pricing, vendor chasing, customer clarification or approval?
5. How long did the last three RFQs take? Can they show sanitized examples?
6. What existing automation solves this, and where does it fail?
7. What is the cost of a missed or incorrect quote and who approves software budget?
8. Would they trial a narrow benchmark? Under what data/security terms?
9. Is this urgent enough for a paid pilot, rather than just an interesting demo?

## Outreach rules

- Use a **personal, separately authorized business identity**, never employer-owned Gmail or data.
- Outreach drafts must be truthful: "I am exploring/building", **not** "I built" or unmeasured time-saving claims.
- Review every target and message before sending; no auto-enrollment/auto-sending.
- Respect local consent/marketing rules, recipient opt-out requests and suppression lists.
- Use accurate subject, identity and contact information. Never scrape gated LinkedIn data contrary to platform rules.
- Track sent date, evidence URL, recipient role, message, reply category and explicit unsubscribe.
- Stop follow-up on any objection/opt-out. Avoid fake urgency, fabricated referrals or guessed emails.
- Don't claim research-qualification scores are conversions or interest signals.

## Sample short outreach

Subject: Question about your RFQ workflow

Hi [First name],

I noticed [Company] accepts [verifiable RFQ/BOM intake detail from URL].

I'm exploring a tool to turn incoming component RFQs into an editable quote-preparation sheet, with staff approving every part match and commercial detail.

Does your team still spend meaningful time manually transcribing and checking those requests, or is it already streamlined in your current system?

Thanks,
Ayush

## CRM outcomes

- Positive discovery interest
- Pain confirmed but not now
- Existing solution handles it
- Workflow mismatch / too few RFQs
- Security/trust objections
- No budget or no authority
- Referral to correct owner
- No response
- Unsubscribe / hard bounce (suppress)

Measure positive **buyer discovery** and paid pilot willingness, not email open rates or a magic response-rate target.

## Pilot hypothesis

An optional small paid proof-of-value using 10–20 **sanitized historical** RFQs and an approved item-master sample. Agree scope and a fixed fee with buyer; do not present $1,500 as validated pricing. Success should include grounded line-item quality, fewer unflagged errors, visible human review, and measured reduction in **actual human time** on comparable work.

Before real files: written authorization, processor disclosure, retention/deletion agreement, no unauthorized email or ERP access, and a confidentiality boundary.

## Go / reconsider

GO to narrow v0 if several independent operators confirm the same expensive pre-quote step and at least one agrees to share test input or pay for a benchmark. RECONSIDER if pain is mostly supplier response time, data integration is a prerequisite, existing systems solve extraction, or small distributors have too little volume.

A paid customer is stronger evidence than a 100-page research report.
